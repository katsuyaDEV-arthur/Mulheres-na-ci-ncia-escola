01 / The pioneers

Meet the scientists

Working in different centuries, both scientists challenged assumptions and helped reshape modern astrophysics.

Black holes & galactic astronomy

Andrea Ghez

Born: June 16, 1965 · New York City, USA

Andrea Ghez is an American astrophysicist known for studying the center of our galaxy. Using high-resolution observations of stars near the Milky Way's center, her research group helped provide compelling evidence for a supermassive black hole called Sagittarius A*.

2020

Nobel Prize in Physics, shared with Reinhard Genzel and Roger Penrose.

Stellar chemistry & astrophysics

Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin

Born: May 10, 1900 · Wendover, England

Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin (1900–1979) was a pioneering astronomer who demonstrated in her 1925 doctoral thesis that stars are made predominantly of hydrogen and helium. Her work overturned earlier assumptions that stars had a chemical composition similar to Earth's.

1925

The year of her groundbreaking doctoral thesis on stellar atmospheres.

02 / Their discoveries

How they changed astronomy

Andrea Ghez

Evidence for a supermassive black hole

By tracking stars orbiting an invisible, extremely compact object at the center of the Milky Way, Ghez and her team measured how gravity affects their motion. The observations provided strong evidence that a supermassive black hole lies there.

  • Studied stellar orbits near Sagittarius A*.
  • Used advanced infrared imaging techniques.
  • Helped test gravity in an extreme environment.
Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin

What are stars made of?

Payne applied atomic physics to the spectra of stars. She showed that the strength of spectral lines depends on temperature and ionization, not just how much of an element is present. This led to the revolutionary conclusion that hydrogen and helium dominate stellar matter.

  • Connected stellar spectra with atomic physics.
  • Identified hydrogen as the most abundant element in stars.
  • Laid foundations for modern stellar astrophysics.
A key idea

Gravity reveals what we cannot see

A black hole itself emits no light, but nearby stars can reveal its presence through their orbital motion.

A key idea

Light reveals what stars contain

By splitting starlight into a spectrum, astronomers can identify chemical elements and learn about the physical conditions of stars.

Payne completes her PhD at Radcliffe College, showing that stars are composed mainly of hydrogen and helium.

Payne becomes the first woman promoted to full professor in Harvard's Faculty of Arts and Sciences.

Ghez and her collaborators use powerful telescopes to follow stars orbiting the Milky Way's central compact object.
